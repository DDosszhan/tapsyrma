## Output Devices, Sensors and Actuators

### Learning goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to choose output devices, match sensors to the data they capture and a suitable use, and explain how a controller drives an actuator.

### Why this matters

Computers communicate results using output devices, but they can also interact with the physical world. Sensors provide measurements; actuators produce physical changes.

### Step 1: Output devices

An output device presents information or a result from the computer. Speakers produce sound, screens display images, printers produce printed output, and actuators cause physical movement or action.

### Step 2: Printers and 3D printing

Inkjet printers spray ink droplets; laser printers use electrostatic attraction and toner; 3D printers build objects layer by layer from digital designs.

### Step 3: Displays and projectors

LCD screens modulate light through liquid crystals; LED screens typically use LEDs as a light source (often an LED-backlit LCD). LCD and DLP projectors use different image-forming technologies.

### Step 4: Sensors

Sensors detect a physical property and provide input. Select one by what it measures and where it will be used. Temperature sensors measure temperature for greenhouse control; humidity sensors measure water vapour in air for ventilation; light sensors measure illumination for automatic lamps; moisture sensors measure water in soil for irrigation; pH sensors measure acidity or alkalinity for water-quality checks.

Gas sensors detect a gas for leak alarms; flow sensors measure how quickly a fluid moves through a pipe; pressure sensors measure force per area for tyre monitoring; level sensors detect the height of liquid in a tank. Accelerometers measure acceleration for phone orientation; proximity sensors detect nearby objects for parking alerts; infra-red sensors detect infrared radiation for movement alarms; magnetic-field sensors detect a field for door alarms; acoustic sensors detect sound for noise monitoring.

### Step 5: Sensor to actuator

A controller can compare sensor readings with a target and then activate an actuator, such as a motor opening a ventilation flap.

[diagram:control-loop]

### Worked example 1: Greenhouse controller

A temperature sensor reports 34°C. The controller compares it with a threshold of 30°C and switches on a fan actuator to cool the greenhouse.

### Worked example 2: Printing a display model

A school wants a physical model of a bridge: a 3D printer builds an object in layers, whereas an inkjet printer produces a two-dimensional printed image.

### Common mistakes

Tip: Sensors provide measurements as input to a controller; they are not output devices.

Tip: An actuator responds to a control signal and changes the environment; a sensor measures it.

Tip: LED-backlit LCD screens differ from displays whose pixels emit their own light.

### Quick recap

Output informs or acts; sensors measure; actuators act. Choose an output device or sensor by the task and the environment.
