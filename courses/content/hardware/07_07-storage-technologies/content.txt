## How Magnetic, Optical and Solid-State Storage Work

### Learning goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to describe magnetic, optical and solid-state storage and choose a device by speed, capacity, cost and durability.

### Why this matters

A hard disk, a Blu-ray disc and a USB flash drive can all store files, but each uses a different physical technology. Understanding how data is recorded helps you select suitable storage.

### Step 1: Magnetic storage

A hard disk drive has rotating platters arranged into tracks and sectors. A read/write head uses magnetic effects to read and write data.

### Step 2: Optical storage

Optical discs such as CD, DVD and Blu-ray are read using lasers that detect differences in reflected light from pits and lands. Optical media can be useful for distribution or archives.

[diagram:three-storage]

### Step 3: Solid-state storage

Flash memory stores charge in cells made from transistors, commonly using floating-gate or similar structures; NAND and NOR flash are named in the syllabus. SSDs, SD cards and USB flash drives use solid-state technology.

### Step 4: Comparing devices

HDDs provide substantial capacity at relatively low cost, but mechanical motion makes them vulnerable to shocks. SSDs offer fast access and no moving mechanical parts. Optical discs require a suitable drive.

### Step 5: Choosing storage

Consider capacity, portability, durability, speed, cost and whether data needs to be rewritten.

[diagram:storage-choice]

### Worked example 1: School video archive

For many terabytes of video at low cost, an HDD may be suitable. For fast loading of a laptop operating system, an SSD is often preferable.

### Worked example 2: Offline distribution

A school has 100 computers without reliable internet and compatible drives. An optical disc could distribute read-only materials, while a USB flash drive might be more convenient if rewriting is required.

### Common mistakes

Tip: An SSD stores data in flash cells without spinning platters.

Tip: A laser reads patterns on an optical disc; the disc is not read magnetically.

Tip: A USB connector does not tell you how the device stores data; check the storage technology itself.

### Quick recap

HDD = magnetic rotating platters; optical = laser/pits/lands; flash = non-volatile transistor cells without moving storage parts.
